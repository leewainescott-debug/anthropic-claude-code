# Lee's way of building a slide

The portable part - how the story flows, how the page is laid out, how it
reads, how the so-what lands. This is the standard. It applies to any client,
any topic, any palette.

Client-specific paint (fonts, brand colours) is an appendix at the bottom and
gets swapped per client. Do not confuse the skin for the method.

Worked example of every principle below: `reference/Ampol_FY27_Budget_Update_FINAL.pptx`
(his finalised deck, 18/08).

---

## 1. The so-what is the title

The title is a **full sentence carrying the number and its consequence**. Never
a topic label ("FY26 Forecast"), never a bare number. If someone reads only the
titles, they have the whole argument.

The pattern is **[position, with the number] + [what it means / what depends on
it / what we do about it]**:

| His title | Position | Attached so-what |
|---|---|---|
| "TDD is currently tracking 320k over the TDD lights on budget **with vacancy and offshore controls available to bring TDD back within budget**" | 320k over | and here is the fix |
| "FY26 forecasts TDD to land $400k above the $53.8m allocated Lights on budget; **assuming all vacancies are hired as expected**" | $400k above | and here is the condition |
| "82 roles and 9 squads have been identified for offshoring, **providing an estimated $8.4m cost reduction** to the overall TDD people cost" | 82 roles, 9 squads | and here is the value |
| "Vacancy controls will be used to determine when and how vacancies are recruited in the new operating model" | the mechanism | and what it governs |

Bad news is stated plainly in the title, with the response attached. Never bury
it in the body and never dress it up.

## 2. How the story flows across the deck

Answer first, then earn it. His running order:

1. **Title** - what this is about.
2. **Executive summary** - the entire argument, in three numbered columns. The
   reader can stop here and be correct.
3. **What changed** - the model, drawn as From → To.
4. **Proof it works** - the structural evidence (spans and layers).
5. **Where we stand now** - the current cost position.
6. **Where we are heading** - the forecast.
7. **The lever that controls it** - vacancy controls.
8. **The second lever** - offshoring.

The rule underneath: **each slide answers the question the previous slide
raises.** Cost is over → what will you do → here is lever one → here is lever
two. Never a slide that exists because the data existed.

Numbering the exec summary 1 / 2 / 3 tells the reader exactly how many things
there are. Use it.

## 3. How the page reads - left to right, top to bottom

The eye path is deliberate:

```
        TITLE = the answer, full width, read first
   ┌──────────────────────────────┬──────────────┐
   │  EVIDENCE                    │  MEANING     │
   │  tables, charts              │  Insight &   │
   │  ~70% of the width           │  Assumptions │
   │                              │  ~25%        │
   │  the numbers themselves      │  what to     │
   │                              │  believe     │
   └──────────────────────────────┴──────────────┘
     footnotes = the basis, small, bottom
```

- **Left is evidence, right is interpretation.** Numbers live on the left where
  the eye lands first; the right rail tells you what they mean and what was
  assumed. Assumptions are never hidden - they get their own named column.
- **Left to right also means earlier to later, or before to after.** From on the
  left, To on the right. Current state left, future state right. Never reversed.
- **Top to bottom means broad to specific.** On his FY26 slide: gross cost table
  on top (everything), lights-on table beneath (what actually lands). On his
  vacancy slide: the decision table on top (what), the phasing chart beneath
  (when).
- Two tables stack rather than sit side by side when the second is derived from
  the first - the reader follows the derivation downward.

## 4. Layers of detail - a reader can stop anywhere

Five levels, each deeper, each independently correct:

1. **Title** - one sentence, the whole message.
2. **Kicker** - one navy line above each table: what this table is, its units,
   its basis. e.g. *"GROSS, $m AUD: the model price of everyone on board,
   ramping with the hire months"*. No table is ever left ambiguous.
3. **The table or chart** - the numbers.
4. **Footnotes** - numbered, small, bottom: the caveat and the definition.
5. **Speaker notes** - the full spoken narrative, conclusion first, then the
   evidence, then what it means.

Someone reading for five seconds gets the title. Someone reading for five
minutes gets all five. Both come away with the same conclusion.

## 5. How numbers are written

- **Always paired with what they are measured against.** "$54.2m vs a budget of
  $53.8m". "24% to 16%". "4.2 to 6.3". "from 420 FTE to 517 FTE". A naked number
  is never enough.
- **Every table resolves** - always a total row, always labelled ("Total",
  "All roles", "All open vacancies", "Nine squads"), never left to the reader to
  add up.
- Brackets for negatives. Red only where something is over. Consistent decimals.
- **TBC where a number genuinely is not known** - never a dash, never a guess,
  never a plug.
- Callout numbers (1, 2, 3) link a specific cell to a specific insight bullet -
  spatial linking, so the explanation sits next to the number it explains rather
  than being described in prose.

## 6. Honesty devices, used on purpose

These are part of the design, not decoration:
- The **assumptions rail** - named, on every content slide, so the basis travels
  with the number.
- **"Draft for discussion"** flag on anything not yet final.
- **Footnotes** carrying the definition of a line, not marketing.
- **TBC**, brackets, and red-for-over as above.
- Bad news in the title.

## 7. Density

Dense but layered - not one-idea-per-slide, and not a wall. A slide carries a
table (or two), a chart, and a rail of interpretation, and still reads cleanly
because the hierarchy is strict: title → kicker → numbers → footnote. Fill the
page with substance, never with decoration.

## 8. Slide shapes he uses

- **Content slide** (the workhorse) - title, evidence left, Insight &
  Assumptions right, footnotes bottom. Section 3 above.
- **Executive summary** - three numbered columns, header block on each, bullets
  with **bold lead-ins** so the page can be skimmed by reading only the bold.
- **From / To** - two halves, before on the left, after on the right, the right
  half sitting on a tinted panel so the change is visible at a glance. Annotation
  boxes carry what changed; markers flag the blocks that move.
- **Section / narrative** - full-height coloured panel down one side carrying
  the title and the story in prose, content tables on the open side.
- **Title slide** - full-bleed photograph, logo, title. Nothing else.

## 9. Charts

Native and editable, never a picture of a chart. Chosen for the job:
- **Waterfall** to bridge one position to another (full price → each lever →
  landing), value labelled on every step.
- **Columns + cumulative line** for phasing, with a dashed divider between the
  two years, direct labels on every point, and a one-line caption under the
  chart saying what the reader is looking at.
- Direct labels beat legends. Axis furniture stays minimal. Zero baselines.

## 10. Language

- **Sentence case everywhere.** Not Title Case, not caps.
- Short, plain, declarative. No consulting throat-clearing, no adjectives doing
  work that a number should do.
- **Banned: saving / savings / saves / saved** - it is a **cost reduction** or
  **cost avoidance**. Also banned: wave, seat, floor, roster, Category as a
  column label, em and en dashes as sentence punctuation, dashes as cell filler,
  "theirs/their" for Finance.
- "charged to" and "recharged to" are his words and are fine.
- Terms he uses: lights on · landing · gross · significant items · overheads ·
  vacancy controls · right shore · offshore / hybrid · spans and layers ·
  management density · span of control · portfolio / platform / squad · FTE ·
  Op-Model · cost avoidance · cost reduction · recharge · allocation ·
  delta to budget · over/(under) · archetype · TBC

# Appendix - client skin (Ampol only, swap per client)

Not the standard. The proportions above are what carry over; these are the
specific values his Ampol deck happens to use.

**Canvas** 13.33 x 7.5in (16:9).

**Fonts** Mark OT Book (theme), MarkOT (body), MarkOTBold; Arial fallback in
tables and charts.

**Palette** navy `18249C` primary · red `ED0C06` rationed to over-budget
numbers, callout circles and the cumulative line · grey `5F5F5F` · `E0E0E0` ·
coral `F76D69` markers · periwinkle `747BC3` for the draft flag · lilac
`C6CAF6` table fills · `F2F2F2` / `E3E3E3` panels and total rows.

**Measured geometry of his content slide** (the source of the ~70/25 split):
| Element | x | y | w |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action title | 0.48 | 0.47 | 12.36 (full width) |
| Evidence column | 0.41-0.48 | 1.34-1.76 | 8.67-9.20 |
| Kicker above a table | 0.41 | above each table | 6.69-7.99 |
| Insight rail | 9.61-9.76 | 1.24-1.40 | 3.26-3.36 |
| Footnote strip | 1.10 | 6.64 | 12.37 |

Sizes: 9pt table text, 10-12pt body and rail, 8pt footnotes, 32pt title.
Tables: coloured header row with white bold text, banded body, labelled total
row. Slide number top right, logo bottom left.

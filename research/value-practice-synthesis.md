# The value practice

1. Clients keep approving investments and cannot see, prove or steer the value coming back; the case goes in a drawer and nobody tracks whether the benefits landed.
2. Value is the extent to which an investment moves the business towards something it already said mattered, and it only counts once someone in the business owns the number and agrees when we will check it.
3. Clients tell us where they stand in six sentences, each carrying its own buyer, date and first purchase.
4. When someone needs a business case approved, we build a value case instead that keeps running long after approval.
5. When a programme has nothing to show, or nobody trusts what it is reporting, we rebaseline what is left and give an honest recommendation.
6. When a client already owns an under utilised platform, we show them what is locked inside it before they buy something new.
7. When a board asks what its AI spend bought, or a deal has closed, the same discipline answers both, in the buyer's own language.
8. Every assessment and value case we build is deliberately assembled from the same four parts, so each door leads one room closer to a standing Value Realisation Office.
9. The office is the flagship we are building towards, wired into the platforms we build on rather than a programme's own account of itself, and sized for clients the big offices have priced out.
10. We go to market through the ears of people already in these accounts, build the practice by reusing the same artefacts on every engagement, and prove it with our own numbers first.

## The problem

Clients keep approving investments and cannot see, prove or steer the value coming back from them; the case is never opened again, delivery reports green throughout, the team disbands at go live, and nobody can say what the business actually got.

Roughly a quarter of a transformation's value is lost at the business case stage before anything is built, and only 0.5 per cent of projects, one in two hundred, ever hit cost, time and benefits together. We say we'll deliver on time and on budget in project management, but the budget is never really the issue if you're not realising the benefits you set out to get. Most organisations do not deliver their full benefits, and plenty of acquisitions have shown green scorecards while shareholder returns fell.

This year the question moved into the boardroom; boards now make further AI funding conditional on proof of return, and board level AI value reporting is expected to be standard by year end. We need to be solving a problem as we can't just create a solution that doesn't solve a business problem. And often we'd need to tell the client with the potential problem. So every conversation this practice opens starts from one question: what value does this investment drive for our business as a whole and how do we set ourselves up to realise it.

## What we mean by value

Before we can sell value we have to say what it is, and our industry has not managed that; every large firm sells value creation and defines it differently, and even the academic literature has no consensus. The definition we use is therefore a decision rather than a discovery.

Value is the extent to which an investment moves the business towards something it already said mattered, a strategic priority rather than a technology outcome; it only counts when someone in the business owns it and agrees when we will check it. Often for technology investments clients are wanting a business case to justify the investment. We need to reframe clients thinking to ensure that the investment drives business value, more than just their own dept or replacing a burning legacy platform, therefore, we need to start thinking about value cases and baselining the investment so that business value is realised beyond implementation. What strategic priorities is this investment driving as opposed to what technology outcome is it driving; allows better long term strategic decision making that will impact the business long term.

Return on investment fails the same way twice; a return can be earned on something that never mattered, and a ratio has no owner to answer when it fails to appear. For transformation programmes, how are we actually realising the value that we set out to achieve, goes beyond roi; but what business kpis are we driving. Value is also not always money; it is defined in the client's own measures.

Every definition that works shares the same mechanics: measurable, baselined, owned by a named person in the business, time bound, tied to strategy, and about outcomes rather than outputs; that is what our two sentences say, and why the pairing beats any single alternative.

## The six moments

We organise the front of this practice by what the client says, not by where an investment sits; each of six sentences carries a different buyer, date and first purchase.

When someone needs a business case approved, that is a sponsoring executive facing a sceptical committee, unable to say how the case stays useful once approved. They buy a value case for one flagship investment, three to six weeks, fixed fee, with a driver tree, baselines and named owners, leading into the install that keeps it alive. They would never buy anything that slows the approval down, or a value office that week.

When a steering committee keeps asking what value is being created, that is the programme sponsor, unable to say what the business has received because the benefits were never baselined. They buy a retrofit of a few weeks, fixed fee, funded from the programme's own budget; we rebaseline what is left, name an owner for each benefit, and wire the numbers into the programme's own reporting, leading into a standing monthly review. They would never buy an audit of their own case.

When they no longer trust what a programme is telling them, the buyer sits above it, often the chief financial officer; what business value can this drive, how are we going to realise it post investment and implementation, is what they cannot answer. They buy a rapid independent value diagnostic, two to four weeks, fixed fee, ending in a rebaselined case and a recommendation to continue, pause, stop or accelerate, and we only take this work where Slalom is not the delivery partner, because independence is the entire product. They would never buy more governance from the lens that already failed them.

When a client pays a great deal for a platform, there are two buyers, the platform owner facing a renewal and the executive whose direction the platform serves. Are you getting the most out of any platform that you're using today is what neither can answer. Owning a capable platform while teams work around it in spreadsheets and still shop for a new tool that does the same job is not a client failing; it is the organisational default; you have already invested in this platform, but it's either under utilised or there are locked benefits or you could be getting so much more out of your investment, and often clients invest in these platforms and haven't even fully unlocked the capabilities they have available to them. They buy a platform value assessment, two to three weeks, fixed fee, reconciling what is contracted, activated and used against the next twelve to twenty four months of business direction, because you're using this platform, your business is going in x direction, there are things we can help you unlock in this platform that will actually drive better outcomes for your business as a whole. This is using strategy as an enabler for our clients to unlock value in their existing platforms without having to pay millions to upgrade their platforms; we do the assessment and use it to prove the value that is locked, and then we clearly outline the roadmap to unlock trapped value in their organisations. They would never buy a free health check dressed up as strategy, or the upgrade they are trying to avoid.

When a board is asking what the business is actually getting for its AI spend, the buyer is the chief financial officer or the executive accountable for AI. AI is part of the conversation, but is not the whole conversation; we are not selling AI strategy or pilots, we are helping clients realise value from AI. They cannot answer what the spend is costing or which initiatives are working. They buy an AI value scan, two to four weeks, fixed fee, inventorying spend against a driver tree and giving a clear view of what to fund, fix or stop, leading into a finance sign off gate. They would never buy another AI strategy, more pilots, or anything that smells like policing their own portfolio.

When a deal has just been signed and now the value has to show up, the buyer is the integration lead or the operating partner, already invested in the outcome. The whole moment is accelerating speed to value; we help clients realise value quickly from their mergers, and we help the owners of portfolios realise value more quickly from their investments. They cannot answer whether the synergy number is real and when it will land, since most acquirers overestimate synergies at announcement and few hit their targets. They buy synergy and value tracking stood up for a live integration, or a hundred day value plan for a newly acquired portfolio company, wired to real systems rather than slides; a corporate acquirer gets a deal value cell that lives and dies with the integration, while a private equity owner gets a standing portfolio value office instead. They would never buy deal strategy from a technology consultancy, or education about why value matters.

## What we sell

The practice packages its work by shape rather than by moment, because a shape can be staffed, priced, reused and turned into a reference; it needs to be super practical and easy to have conversation with our clients on.

The first shape is the assessment: short, sharply scoped, fixed length and fixed fee, staffed by a pod of two or three with a senior person fronting it, covering the platform value assessment, the AI value scan, the deal value check, and a value maturity assessment.

The second shape is the value case and the install that makes it live; business cases are just set up to get the dollars and then go nowhere and aren't living, breathing documents, how do we actually turn those business cases into living and breathing baseline and metric tracking sources that are used throughout the engagement. The install puts the metrics inside the dashboards leaders already review, ends each month in a real decision, and rebaselines the case at every gate.

The third shape is the office itself, sized in the next part.

Who cares, why do they care, what's the easy entry point, is the test every shape has to pass. On money: What's the easy button; any funding they can poke on; partner funding etc. The client pays by default, because a short sharply scoped assessment is carried comfortably by the value it finds; vendor incentives help at the margins but mostly offset our own cost, so this work is bought, not granted.

## What we actually do, every time

Put simply, it's more than just reframing it to a value case, it's how do we ensure that this is driving business value, how do we set it up so that we can show the value delivered and how do we ensure the value is delivered, reported. Four things follow, done on every investment.

We frame the value, building a driver tree from the board level outcome to the operational measures a manager can act on, baselined against real data, with a named owner in the business. We fund it properly, releasing money in tranches against evidence where governance allows it. We track it live, putting the metrics inside the dashboards leaders already review rather than a spreadsheet that decays, wiring them to the platform's own usage data wherever we built it. And we govern it to realisation, with a fixed cadence, a real decision at the end, and one senior owner who can stop something.

These four things run across the whole life of an investment, from before the money moves until long after delivery has disbanded, which is why one practice serves six conversations; value gets framed early, protected while the work runs, and proved once live, and a standing office does all three at once, permanently.

We name the failure modes in front of the client, because naming them makes this credible: benefit tracking theatre, where the dashboard moves but nobody owns it; post project amnesia, where the team disbands with it; the wallpaper business case nobody has opened since approval; ownership by implication, a benefit with no name against it; and a value office that behaves like police instead of a coach.

Every assessment and value case we build is deliberately assembled out of the same four parts, a baseline, a driver tree, an owner map and a cadence, so every engagement leaves the client one room closer to the office whether they buy it or not.

## The Value Realisation Office

This whole practice was named in one sentence: setting up a vro, doing the initial business case but framing it differently so it's not just a business case that gets money in but business cases that help realise value, if not setting up the vro it could be setting up value tracking mechanisms and reporting cadence and governance to truly support value delivery. The office is the flagship that sentence points to.

A Value Realisation Office is a small standing team that makes value somebody's actual job, reporting to the board and anchored to finance. It stands up in three to six months, with early wins inside the first ninety days, growing by proving value, and we build it with the client, run it together and hand it over, because an office the client can run is a reference and one they depend on is a liability.

Every large firm sells a version of this category, proven and bought, so the question is what makes ours different. It is where the numbers come from; incumbent offices run on manually reported programme status, while we sit inside the AWS, Salesforce, Snowflake and Databricks build and wire the office to what the platforms already record, holding one value picture across a client's whole estate. And because hand collected reporting takes people, incumbent offices are priced for very large programmes, while ours can be small enough for a mid sized organisation to actually afford.

There is one client this office answers directly, the one who cannot say what value means for them or who wants decisions tied to strategy rather than whoever argued loudest in the funding round; even there the first purchase stays small, a maturity assessment across the portfolio; we need to position ourselves to have broader client conversations with more than just technology. How do we position ourselves as truly value led. Value is such a broad topic, but we need to make it easier for clients to capture value, to have the conversation on value, to realise value. The office is that ambition made structural: what value does this investment drive for our business as a whole and how do we set ourselves up to realise it.

## Going to market

A practice of one person does not generate its own pipeline; setting up the client partners and internal teams well so they're able to bring me into a conversation to discuss with our clients on business value is the whole go to market. The six moments become one listening sheet: the sentence, who is saying it and why now, the line back, and the small first purchase, so a client partner's job ends with one line and a phone call.

One person cannot push six doors at once, so we lead with the AI moment and the platform moment, where boards are asking now and renewals and bills are landing already. The other moments ride the delivery relationships we already hold, and the office is pulled rather than pushed.

The vendor partners matter twice, and the honest order is doors before money; their value teams build the case before the sale and stop mattering after close, leaving delivery owned by nobody, which is where we stand. Salesforce pays for adoption after sale, Databricks ties assessments to tiering, Snowflake gates its fund by tier, and AWS runs reviews free, but the money sits at the margins, offsetting our cost rather than the client's bill.

Our relationships mostly live on the technology side while value gets verified in finance, so moments one, two and four enter through relationships we already hold, while moments three and five and the office itself walk the conversation to the chief financial officer; every sponsor side engagement is judged on that finance conversation.

## Building the practice

The practice builds by reusing the same artefacts on every engagement: the value case template, the assessment run sheet, the tracking pack, and the office starter kit. Reuse is the whole point; a pod of two or three with a senior person fronting it can run any shape, and if every engagement stays bespoke then I am the product and the practice cannot outlive my calendar.

A young practice cannot lean on public statistics, since they all need caveats, so every fixed shape engagement banks its own quantified before and after, and those references are what make the office sellable; what do clients actually buy and want to pay for vs hypothetical decides what gets built next, and a second senior person joins to run a lead moment once an entry shape has converted more than once.

The same evidence discipline points further out. The market is moving fees onto outcome based terms and stalling because nobody can substantiate the outcome, and access to the platform's own usage data is what makes substantiation possible; a fee genuinely at risk against verified value is proof in place of another promise about time and budget. It goes to no client until Slalom decides it has the appetite, since pricing against outcomes is a firm level decision; the first step today is holding everything we publish, this document included, to that standard.

## The first ninety days

What I am asking for now is deliberately small: an hour with each account team to teach the listening sheet, because it only works if the sentences come back unprompted; one named account for each lead moment; agreement to publish the two entry assessments at a fixed duration and fixed fee, since no competitor publishes one; and the existing AI value forecasting tool as the base for the tracking layer the market now expects. There is no headcount ask until the conversion evidence exists. In the first ninety days the platform assessment and the AI value scan should both be running in known accounts, fronted by me, with every artefact codified for reuse and a quantified before and after banked from each.

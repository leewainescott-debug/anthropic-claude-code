# The thematics of the research

What the body of research on value realisation, value architecture and value framing says on its own terms, independent of any one argument. Compiled 19 August 2026 from the research files in this folder, where every theme's sources and confidence flags live.

1. Realisation is where value dies, and it is measured. The loss concentrates at target setting and after go live rather than during delivery; delivery discipline improved for decades while benefits discipline never did.

2. Ownership is the field's most repeated finding. Across the benefits discipline, the transformation studies and deal practice, a benefit with a named owner in the business arrives and an unowned one does not; ownership beats method every time it is tested.

3. Measurement decays unless it lives inside existing rhythms. Separate benefits tracking dies within months; the only tracking that survives sits inside dashboards and meetings leaders already attend.

4. Two schools answer the same problem. The measurement school says value fails for lack of baselines and tracking; the change school says it fails because behaviour never changed, with adoption and workflow redesign carrying most of the outcome. The strongest practices run both; most firms sell only one.

5. The definition of value was never settled. Financial value is the only dimension every authority names; the public sector and the benefits discipline treat customer, people, risk and societal value as equals; the field splits on whether to monetise everything into one number or judge a scorecard in native units, and on whether you measure the technology or the business.

6. Optimism bias is institutionalised. Governments formally inflate every business case to correct for it; the field treats case inflation as a law of nature to be corrected upstream, which quietly concedes that cases as written cannot be trusted.

7. The value office is the market's converged product. Nearly every large firm sells the same shape, a diagnostic, then a value tree, then an embedded office anchored to finance, with value leakage as the shared enemy and a tooling layer now expected; fees are drifting toward outcomes and stalling on proof.

8. Vendors run value machinery that stops at signature. Value engineering exists at every major platform to win the sale; after close, nobody owns realisation, and that gap is visible across every vendor studied.

9. Governance turned in 2026. Boards now gate further funding on proof of return, value reporting is heading to board standard, and visibility plus accountability measurably multiply realised value; the question moved from the programme office to the boardroom.

10. The lifecycle is the discipline's native shape. Identify, execute, sustain; plan, in flight, post implementation; opportunity through steady state: every serious body of work organises realisation across the life of an investment with a standing function above it.

11. Underuse is the quiet evidence base. Half of licensed capability sits unused, teams run work in spreadsheets beside platforms that could do it, cloud waste is rising again, and the cost discipline that grew up around this is now widening into a value discipline.

12. Deals are the most quantified arena. Synergy overestimation is systemic, cost synergies land while revenue synergies mostly do not, speed of integration correlates with capture, and the hundred day operating pattern is the most mature realisation practice the field has produced.

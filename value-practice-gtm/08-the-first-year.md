# The first year

A new practice takes root on proof concentration and incentive engineering, and dies on generic positioning, unfunded patience and the assumption that account teams will sell it out of goodwill. That is the compressed finding of the practice building research, and the first year plan below is built directly on it, adjusted for the two facts that make Slalom specific: the local market structure fragments any national launch, and the concept already exists inside Slalom as scattered assets with no single owner.

## The claim, the face and the product

The practice makes one narrow claim, value realisation for platform investments, because a narrow expert claim wins ground a broad strategy claim cannot, and a broad claim invites comparison with MBB on ground where MBB wins. It launches with one visible expert fronting the point of view, because the research finds firms with named visible experts measurably accelerate growth, and a practice needs a face more than it needs a service sheet. And it launches with the published fixed price value case as its product, because packaged offers now make up a quarter of professional services revenue and a fixed scope, fixed fee, named deliverable is what reduces the buying friction of a thing nobody has bought before.

## Lighthouse clients on charter terms

The first year sales plan is two or three named lighthouse accounts, as opposed to pipeline arithmetic, recruited from accounts where Slalom is already delivering on one of the four platforms and where a live investment is approaching a gate, a renewal or a board question. Charter terms are formal and time boxed: a stated discount, in the standard range of 20 to 50 per cent, traded for contractual case study, reference and named number rights, because an informal discount buys nothing and the practice's entire future pricing power depends on owned proof. The charter engagements should deliberately span at least two arenas and two platforms, so the proof base supports the cross platform claim rather than a single ecosystem story.

## The internal go to market, engineered rather than hoped for

The documented failure mode for new offerings inside consultancies is that seller doers have delivery obligations that crowd out selling the new thing, and incumbent controls act as antibodies against anything unproven. Four mechanisms answer this. First, an executive sponsor who owns the profit and loss exception, so the practice is not managed to quarterly utilisation in the period when its job is building proof; the industry benchmark backdrop is that even mature practices run about 66 per cent utilisation, and a year one practice must be allowed to run lower while it concentrates on lighthouse proof. Second, incentive asymmetry as a deliberate temporary subsidy: the introducing account team keeps full revenue credit, double counted if the systems require it, because a practice that competes with its own account teams for credit starves. Third, written rules of engagement covering who leads which conversation, how the value case attaches to platform proposals, and how assurance referrals hand over, with dedicated pursuit support from the practice on the first ten deals. Fourth, consolidation of the existing estate: the Business Value Framework, the AI business value calculator, the DealRoom Synergy Tracker and the outcome quantified case studies move under the practice as its founding asset base, which both accelerates the launch and settles the who owns this question before it becomes turf war.

The alliance argument should be made explicitly in the internal case: all four ecosystems now pay for evidenced consumption and adoption, Slalom has just signed a four year collaboration agreement with AWS promising exactly the adoption and differentiation outcomes this practice manufactures, and Salesforce's 2026 program overhaul directs roughly a billion dollars of incentives toward demonstrated customer value. The practice is therefore an alliance economics asset for the whole firm, and that argument lands in a cost scrutiny environment where a headcount led pitch would not.

## The practice's own value case

The practice runs on its own method from day one, with baselines, owners and dates on one page. The recommended year one targets: three lighthouse engagements delivered with finance verified results and contractual reference rights; a published point of view and a published fixed price offer live in the first quarter; ten value case diagnostics sold across the year with conversion tracked against the one in three benchmark; at least one value office stood up by month twelve on build, operate and transfer terms; partner sourced funding captured on at least two engagements; and the telemetry adapters built for at least two of the four platforms. Funding is an eighteen month investment case with explicit cash tolerance, because that is the modelled norm for practice breakeven, and pretending otherwise sets the practice up to be killed at month nine for missing a number it was never going to hit.

## The first ninety days

Name the sponsor and agree the profit and loss exception in writing. Consolidate the existing value assets under the practice. Publish the point of view and the fixed price offer. Build the benefit quality gate and the first value tree so the diagnostic is deliverable, and run the first executive briefings inside three accounts the platform leads nominate. Sign the first charter client. Put the practice's own value case on one page and review it monthly with the sponsor, going, holding, killing or accelerating its own initiatives on the same cadence it will sell to clients.

## The pre mortem

If this practice is dead in twelve months, one of five things killed it. It got sold as staff augmentation, senior people placed into programmes with the method left behind; the defence is that the practice sells offers with named deliverables and declines body requests. It sold offices without decision rights and became reporting theatre; the defence is the rule that an office carries authority or is not sold. The account teams never sold it; the defence is the incentive asymmetry and rules of engagement above, checked monthly. It became an AI practice by drift because AI demand was loudest, and died when the cycle turned; the defence is the arena discipline, with charter proof deliberately spread across arenas. Or it proved nothing, because charter deals were discounted without reference rights and the first engagements skipped their own baselines; the defence is that the practice's proof system applies to itself, and the sponsor's monthly review asks for the practice's own verified numbers first.

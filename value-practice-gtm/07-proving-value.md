# Proving value

Proving value is a design problem at the start of an engagement, as opposed to a reporting problem at the end of one, and this is the muscle the whole market lacks. Selling value and proving value are different muscles precisely because proof depends on decisions, baselines, owners, instrumentation and agreed rules of attribution, that can only be taken before the change begins; a practice that waits until after go live to think about proof has already lost the baseline, and with it the ability to say anything finance will sign. Every engagement in this practice therefore ships with the proof system below built in, and the system is the same in every offer, which is what makes proof compound across engagements into the practice's own evidence base.

## The proof system, in eight parts

**One, the baseline is taken before the change, with the measurement method fixed at case time.** A benefit without a pre change baseline is unprovable forever, and a method chosen after the fact is a method chosen to flatter. Wherever the investment touches AWS, Salesforce, Snowflake or Databricks, the baseline comes from the platform's own usage, adoption and cost data rather than from surveys or self report.

**Two, every benefit has a named owner inside the operating business, and finance verifies.** Ownership is the single most repeated finding in the field: benefits with named owners arrive and unowned ones do not, and a dedicated value owner captures 24 per cent more of a transformation's value on Bain's evidence. Verification runs on a monthly cadence with the finance function, because finance sign off is what separates value that is real from value claimed by whoever built the case, and because a number finance has booked is a number the CFO will repeat to the board.

**Three, the measurement is wired into the platforms.** This is the value plumbing that differentiates the practice: telemetry adapters per platform feeding a value scoreboard, adoption analytics standing in as evidence of behaviour change, and cost visibility feeding unit economics, where the KPMG finding applies that full cost visibility makes an established return five times more likely. Wiring also makes tracking cheap enough to survive, which matters because the field's consistent finding is that separate benefits tracking decays within months while measures living inside dashboards and meetings leaders already attend persist.

**Four, leading indicators run ahead of lagging capture.** The field splits into a measurement school and a behaviour school, and the strongest evidence says value needs both, with 70 per cent of AI value coming from redesigned people and process. The proof system resolves the split structurally, the way Cranfield's dependency network does: adoption, usage and cycle time behave as leading indicators inside the benefit chain, and financial capture follows as the lagging end of the same chain, so behaviour change is measured as part of proof rather than argued about beside it.

**Five, attribution is agreed at signing.** The research on outcome contracting is unambiguous that deals die on attribution disputes, so every engagement carries an attribution protocol agreed before work starts: what counts toward each benefit, how external conditions are handled, how a missed target gets adjudicated between contractor performance, client action and the world. This protocol is also the asset that later makes fees at risk possible on terms the practice controls.

**Six, tracking and evaluation are kept distinct.** Tracking answers whether we got what we promised; evaluation answers what actually caused what. The UK's 2025 evaluation guidance formalised exactly this distinction, and blurring it is how firms end up claiming causation their data cannot carry. The practice tracks always, evaluates deliberately on the benefits that justify the effort, and says plainly which is which.

**Seven, the system defends against its own corruption.** Goodhart's law, that a measure becoming a target stops being a good measure, applies with full force to a tracked benefit tied to funding or bonuses, and the documented failure mode is gaming, from creative classification to outright fiction, which is how deals fail while their scorecards stay green. The defences are structural: benefit evidence is separated from individual incentives, no single metric stands alone where a benefit can be triangulated, some benefits stay qualitative by design on the Cranfield ladder, gatekeepers reviewing kill decisions are independent of the teams being reviewed, and smart kills are celebrated so bad news travels; a value review that only ever says green is evidence of gaming, as opposed to evidence of success.

**Eight, the case dies on purpose and the proof survives it.** After go live and the realisation review, each benefit transfers into its owner's everyday scorecard and the value case is retired. The transfer is the final proof: value has become operational when the business tracks it as its own number without a project artefact or a consultant attached. An engagement that ends with the client unable to run the discipline alone has failed at exactly the point the market always fails.

## The anti patterns, named to clients directly

Naming failure modes is itself a credibility signal, and five are worth naming in any first conversation: benefit tracking theatre, dashboards of vanity metrics nobody signed up to own; post project amnesia, the team disbanding at go live with nobody left holding the benefit; the wallpaper business case, signed at approval and irrelevant by month two; ownership by implication, a benefit with no name, no baseline and no consequence attached; and the value office as police, which gets gamed or ignored when the entire point is to help owners hit their number.

## Proving the practice itself

The practice must run on its own method or deserve the scepticism it meets. That means a value case for the practice with baselines, owners and dates before the first client engagement; contractual reference and case study rights written into every charter deal so proof is ownable; a public scoreboard of verified results, in clients' numbers with clients' names where rights allow; and the discipline of publishing the method, because a practice selling proof cannot hold its own evidence to a lower standard than it demands of a client's business case.

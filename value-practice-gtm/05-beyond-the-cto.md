# Beyond the CTO

The route to the rest of the executive team runs through the technology relationship, as opposed to around it. The instinct behind the question assumes the CTO relationship is a limitation to be escaped; the evidence says it is the asset the whole expansion is built on, because the technology executive currently has the sharpest version of the problem this practice solves. Seventy four per cent of CIOs believe their role is at risk within two years if they cannot demonstrate measurable gains from AI, and nearly all report that board pressure for proof has increased; meanwhile 59 per cent of CFOs and 61 per cent of CIOs each claim primary responsibility for technology investment decisions, so the two offices are colliding over accountability with no shared machinery between them. A practice that helps the CIO build a value story finance will actually sign has, in one motion, served its existing relationship and created its introduction upward, with the CIO as sponsor rather than gatekeeper.

## The route, one relationship at a time

**The CIO or CTO is the first buyer.** The opening service to them is the value case on an investment they already own, framed honestly: the board is going to ask you for proof, and the case you have will not produce it. This is not a pivot away from technology work; it is the work that protects the technology work's funding.

**The CFO enters through verification, as opposed to through a pitch.** The proof system requires finance to verify what counts, monthly, against baselines finance has seen; the strongest value office models in the market anchor to finance for exactly this reason, and McKinsey's own guidance is that a transformation office without CEO and CFO sponsorship devolves into a reporting function. So the CFO's first contact with the practice is a working session where finance co authors the verification rules, which makes the CFO a stakeholder in the machinery rather than an audience for a sales meeting. The demand side is already prepared: CFOs are raising technology spend while their teams report little proven value, Gartner is publicly coaching CFOs to demand portfolio level value governance, and the one datum most worth carrying into that room is that where the CFO is made accountable for AI value, which happens in only 2 per cent of organisations, three quarters report substantial value delivered.

**The COO and every other outcome owner enter because the work forces them to.** Every benefit in a value case must have a named owner inside an operating function, and a baseline that owner accepts; you cannot baseline a warehouse consolidation benefit, a claims cycle benefit or a customer retention benefit without the executive who owns that number in the room. Benefit ownership is therefore relationship architecture: a value case with nine benefits across four functions creates four executive relationships that did not exist, each anchored on a number that executive now owns, and the monthly value review keeps those executives in the same room on a standing cadence. This is the honest answer to how doors get opened: the method opens them as a structural consequence, so the practice never has to ask the CTO for introductions it has not earned.

**The CEO and the board enter through reporting.** Board level value reporting is heading toward standard practice by the end of 2026 on Deloitte's prediction, from 4 per cent of organisations today; the quarterly executive review the office runs is that report, and the practice should design it from the first engagement as something fit to go to the board unedited.

## Matching the door to the arena

Each arena has a natural first executive beyond technology. In the AI arena it is the CFO, who is being asked for numbers they cannot produce. In the platforms already owned arena it is the CFO as cost owner and the COO whose teams run spreadsheets beside the platform. In the deals arena it is the corporate development lead and the integration executive, who own synergy targets that 70 per cent of acquirers miss. In transformations it is whoever the transformation sponsor is, with Bain's finding that a dedicated value owner captures 24 per cent more of the value serving as the argument for creating one where none exists.

## Where the practice should decline to fight

Expansion has a boundary, and respecting it protects credibility. Board altitude value arbitration, meaning enterprise portfolio strategy, multi country programme governance and value numbers that must carry audit or regulatory weight, is ground where brand and assurance heritage win, which means MBB and the Big Four win; the perception research is blunt that clients weight global reach and reputation there. The practice's altitude is where value is made and measured, in the build and in production, and its distinctive sentence to every executive it meets is that we measure value in the systems where it shows up, because we built them. Fighting above that altitude in year one would spend credibility the practice has not yet earned; the route described here earns it.

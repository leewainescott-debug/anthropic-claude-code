# The evidence base

Every figure below has been traced to its primary source in August 2026. Confidence flags follow the original scheme: A means primary and recent, B means primary but older or needing a date caveat, C means secondary and needing verification before client use. The verification pass changed several figures the commissioning research carried, and those corrections come first because using an uncorrected figure in front of a client is the fastest way to lose the credibility this practice sells.

## Corrections the verification pass forced

Three claims should be dropped entirely, because no primary source exists. The claim that fewer than 40 per cent of organisations have a formal process for tracking benefits after go live traces only to a vendor blog citing unnamed PMI research; use PMI's verified maturity figures instead. The claim that 63 to 79 per cent of deals have no integration plan at signing appears fabricated; the real PwC 2023 integration survey figures are that only 43 per cent had a synergy tracking process and 53 per cent set synergy targets. And the claim that only 37 per cent of a vendor's customers have fully embraced its platform was not found anywhere.

Five attributions or phrasings needed fixing. The finding that 66 per cent of boards make further AI funding conditional on proof of return, and that 43 per cent of finance leaders are asked for a return number they cannot produce, belongs to CloudZero's June 2026 survey report, and the same study adds that 87 per cent must tie AI spend to results within a year while only 22 per cent can today; RGP separately found in December 2025 that 66 per cent of CFOs expect significant AI return within two years while only 14 per cent see meaningful value today. The claim that 30 to 50 per cent of merger value is lost to slow integration could not be traced to McKinsey; use instead McKinsey's verified 2011 finding that 50 to 60 per cent of synergy initiatives are strongly related to IT. The 83 per cent of mergers failing to lift shareholder value is KPMG's 1999 study of about seven hundred cross border deals, and the clause about scorecards showing green was embellishment; drop it. The 80 per cent of features figure is Pendo's 2019 report, and the correct phrasing is rarely or never used, as opposed to completely unused. And Bain's 24 per cent finding specifies a dedicated chief transformation officer rather than a generic value owner, from its April 2024 release on a database of more than twenty four thousand initiatives.

Three dates needed fixing. The Gartner CFO figures, 84 per cent of finance organisations adopting AI with only 7 per cent reporting high impact, come from a June 2025 survey of 183 CFOs published in May 2026, and the 62 per cent of CFOs saying fewer than a quarter of AI initiatives deliver measurable benefit is from Gartner's May 2026 finance conference. The Accenture cloud value figure, 42 per cent fully achieving expected outcomes, is research from 2022 to early 2023 rather than January 2025. Wellingtone's benefits figure is 39 per cent in the 2025 edition and 42 per cent in the 2026 edition; cite whichever edition you name.

## Numbers never to use

The banned list stands, with one addition. Never use the claim that 95 per cent of AI pilots fail; it traces to MIT's NANDA report of July 2025, whose sample and definition of failure were widely contested, and if a hook is unavoidable it must be labelled contested and paired with what the successful minority did differently. Never use the claim that 70 per cent of transformations fail, which traces to a recycled 1993 estimate; Bain's 88 per cent figure is the clean replacement. Never state Gartner's 30 per cent generative AI abandonment prediction as an outcome, and label its 40 per cent agentic prediction as a forecast. Never present vendor commissioned return studies as independent evidence; they are usable only as the vendor's own claim of available value. And never use the three dropped claims in the corrections section above, since no primary source exists for any of them.

## Still open

Four items remain unverified and should stay off client slides: the Deloitte phrasing that impact is booked monthly with finance, where the prediction itself is confirmed and the phrasing is not; the Fortune 2 per cent and 76 per cent CFO accountability figures pending the underlying survey; the blended estimate that idle or oversized compute eats 30 to 40 per cent of Snowflake and Databricks spend, which should be verified per client from their own usage data, which is the better sales motion anyway; and hidden cost claims about vendor success tiers, which came from independent advisors rather than primary documents.

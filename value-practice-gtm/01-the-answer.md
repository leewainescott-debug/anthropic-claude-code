# The answer

Slalom should take one claim to market: we are the firm that makes technology investments pay, because we build on the platforms where the value either arrives or fails to arrive, and we wire the evidence of value into those platforms, so that proving value becomes a by product of how the work is delivered rather than a promise made after it. The practice sells the phase of an investment's life that everyone else abandons; the vendors' value engineering teams build the case and leave at signature, the strategy firms track value by hand from outside the build, and the client is left holding a business case nobody intends to revisit. The offers follow the life of an investment: a value case before the money is committed, value assurance while it is being spent, value recovery after go live, and a standing value office for clients ready to own the discipline themselves. AI, platforms already owned, deals and transformations are the four arenas those offers sell into, as opposed to being offers themselves. The entry product is a fixed price, fixed duration value case diagnostic, which no competitor in any tier publishes an equivalent of; the destination is a value office anchored to the CFO and handed over on a build, operate and transfer basis; and the differentiator throughout is that the measurement runs on the platforms' own usage and cost data, which is the one advantage the strategy firms cannot copy and the vendors will not exercise on the client's behalf.

The moment favours this. Boards now gate further AI funding on proof of return, 66 per cent of them by CloudZero's 2026 survey, and 43 per cent of finance leaders are being asked for a return number they cannot produce; all four of Slalom's platform ecosystems restructured their partner economics in 2025 and 2026 to pay for consumption and adoption outcomes, which means the same value evidence earns twice, once as client fees and once as partner incentives; and in Slalom's competitive tier nobody yet owns cross platform value realisation as a named discipline, since Publicis Sapient attached its version to Adobe and West Monroe attached its version to private equity, while the ground across AWS, Salesforce, Snowflake and Databricks, where Slalom holds top partner standing on all four, is open. That window is closing quarter by quarter; Insight Enterprises launched an AI value offering in November 2025 and bought a four hundred person transformation firm to staff it, so the recommendation is to move inside twelve months or accept a crowded market.

## The structure of the problem

The seven questions imply an issue tree with five branches, and each question lands in exactly one of them. A practice that works needs an offer, demand, a motion, proof, and a build; it cannot fail in a way that lands outside those five, and no question below sits in two of them at once, which is the test the tree had to pass before anything else was written.

1. **The offer**, meaning what we sell. This branch holds what value is, since the definition is the foundation of the product; the architecture of the offers and how they relate; and the capability, people, method and assets that deliver them. It answers the first question and the questions about what we sell and what the capability is. The depth sits in `02-what-value-is.md` and `03-what-we-sell.md`.
2. **The demand**, meaning who buys and why now. This branch holds the buyer for each offer, the arenas where demand already exists, and the evidence on what clients actually pay for as opposed to hypothetically want. It answers the sixth question. The depth sits in `06-what-clients-actually-buy.md`.
3. **The motion**, meaning how it gets sold. This branch holds how the conversation opens when the client has not asked, how the conversation changes from technology led to value led, how relationships expand beyond the CTO, and how the work is priced. It answers the second, third, fourth and fifth questions. The depth sits in `04-how-we-sell-it.md` and `05-beyond-the-cto.md`.
4. **The proof**, meaning how delivered value is evidenced. This branch holds the measurement system built into every engagement, the role of finance as the arbiter of what counts, and how proof compounds into references and eventually into fees at risk. It answers the seventh question. The depth sits in `07-proving-value.md`.
5. **The build**, meaning how the practice takes root. This branch holds the first year plan, the internal go to market inside Slalom, the practice's own economics, and the ways this dies if we let it. The depth sits in `08-the-first-year.md`.

Two overlaps had to be resolved for the tree to hold. What clients pay for and how we price sound like one topic; they are kept apart because the first is a market fact we gathered evidence on and the second is a choice we make in light of it. And the three selling questions, opening, reframing and expanding, are stages of one motion rather than separate branches, because treating them separately would hide that each stage creates the conditions for the next.

## The seven answers in brief

**What is value?** Value is a measured improvement in an outcome the business already cares about, expressed in the organisation's own terms, with a baseline taken before the change, a named owner inside the operating business, and a date it is due; it is tied to a strategic priority and framed as an outcome rather than an output, and it never collapses to money alone, though every value case needs a financial spine the CFO can stand behind. The field has not settled a definition, which is an asset for us rather than a problem, because a firm that arrives with a working definition and a quality test for benefits is already teaching. The full definition, the dimensions and the four forks the field disagrees on are in `02-what-value-is.md`.

**How do I sell value?** You sell relief from a proof problem the client already has, as opposed to selling value as an idea, because in 2026 the pressure is concrete: boards are gating AI funding on proof, CFOs are being asked for numbers they cannot produce, and CIOs believe their roles are at risk if they cannot demonstrate measurable gains. The motion is a taught insight, delivered through a published point of view and an executive briefing, converting into a paid, fixed price diagnostic, which the evidence says converts to larger work roughly one time in three. The full motion is in `04-how-we-sell-it.md`.

**How do I go to market with value?** With a published fixed price entry product, one visible expert who fronts the point of view, and two or three lighthouse clients recruited from accounts where Slalom is already delivering, on charter terms that trade a discount for contractual reference and case study rights. The practice launches into the four arenas through the platform relationships it already holds, and it earns partner economics as well as client fees, because all four ecosystems now pay for evidenced consumption and adoption. The launch design is in `04-how-we-sell-it.md` and `08-the-first-year.md`.

**How do I change the conversation to be value led?** By changing the question the investment has to answer, from what will this technology deliver to what will this move in the business and who owns the number. The instrument of that change is the reframe from business case to value case: same document class, different job, where the case names the strategic priorities the investment drives, the measures in the board's own language, the baselines, the owners, the dates, and the conditions under which the investment would be killed. One refinement matters and is argued in full in `09-where-we-disagree.md`: the value case should live through delivery and then die on purpose, with its benefits dissolving into the owners' everyday scorecards, because a case that lives forever becomes a gamed target and a reporting bureaucracy.

**How do I open doors beyond the CTO?** Through the technology relationship, as opposed to around it. The CIO has a board problem the practice solves, so the CIO becomes the first buyer and then the internal sponsor; the CFO enters because the proof system requires finance to verify what counts, and co authoring the verification makes the CFO a stakeholder rather than an audience; the COO and every other outcome owner enter because every benefit in the value case must have a named owner in an operating function, and each named owner is a meeting the work itself forces into existence. The full route is in `05-beyond-the-cto.md`.

**What will clients actually buy?** The evidence says clients pay for measurement attached to money already in motion, and they pay for it bundled or adjacent to delivery rather than as a standalone subscription. Standalone benefits work signs at modest ticket sizes, roughly forty thousand to two hundred and fifty thousand pounds in the only public record of signed contracts; paid diagnostics convert about one in three; embedded value office roles price like senior programme staff; and hybrid fees sign while pure outcome fees stall on attribution. Vendor partner funds pay for assessments, so the entry can be cheap or free to the client while remaining paid work for the practice. The full evidence is in `06-what-clients-actually-buy.md`.

**How do we prove value was delivered?** By designing the proof at the start, since proof attempted after go live fails for want of a baseline. The proof system has eight parts: baselines before change, named owners, finance verification on a fixed cadence, measurement wired into the platforms' own telemetry, leading indicators of behaviour change ahead of lagging financial capture, an attribution protocol agreed at signing, evaluation kept distinct from tracking, and defences against gaming, including keeping some benefits qualitative by design. The system is in `07-proving-value.md`.

## The position we recommend, and the rivals it beat

The recommended identity is value realisation for platform investments, sold across the whole life of an investment, evidenced from the platforms' own data. Four rival positions were considered seriously and lost.

Leading with AI value as the practice's identity lost because AI is an arena rather than an offer; it would date the practice to a funding cycle, invite the scepticism now attaching to AI vendor claims, and surrender the deals, platform and transformation demand that the same discipline serves. AI should be the loudest door the practice walks through in year one, and it should never be the name on the door.

Leading with the value realisation office as the flagship lost because it asks the client for the largest commitment first, on no evidence base, and the market's own converged product shape runs diagnostic first, then value artefact, then office. The office is the destination and the diagnostic is the door.

Positioning as vendor agnostic value assurance in the MBB style lost because that fight happens at board altitude where brand and audit weight win, which is where a Slalom tier firm credibly loses; it would also abandon the platform fluency that is the only structural advantage in this market Slalom holds and MBB lacks. The practice should fight where value is made and measured, in the build and in production.

Pricing on outcomes from day one lost because outcome fees demonstrably stall on attribution and baselines; the peer reviewed evidence says outcome contracts outperform only where outcomes have been made contractible first. The practice should sell the measurement that makes outcomes contractible, take hybrid fees with a capped at risk share once its own baselines exist, and treat fee at risk as a year two earned right rather than a launch feature.

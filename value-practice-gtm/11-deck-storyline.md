# The deck storyline

This is the playback of the slide structure for the internal deck, rebuilt from the twenty nine slide draft, the comments left in the shapes, the research pack, and a fresh research pass on how to measure the true value of an investment when throughput alone cannot be the answer. The deck is an internal sales pitch and an internal training document in one, so it has to do two jobs: convince the room this matters and teach the room to have the conversation. Every slide below has one purpose and one action title making a single claim, quantified where the evidence allows; the seventeen titles read in sequence carry the whole argument without the slides, which is the test they were written against.

## The spine

The deck runs on one question asked three ways, which is the fix for the story being hard to follow. Act one asks what is this and why now, and ends with the reader able to say what value, value architecture and value realisation mean in plain english. Act two asks how does it actually work, and ends with the reader able to explain how an investment becomes value, how we measure it and how we realise it. Act three asks how do we sell it, and ends with the reader knowing the offers, the entry product, the conversation and the plan. The appendix carries every framework detail, so no slide in the main deck is doing appendix work.

The single message of the whole deck, stated on slide two and never left: clients are being asked by their boards to prove what technology investment does for the business, they cannot, and value realisation is the discipline we sell that fixes it, designed up front through value architecture and proven through delivery in the platforms we already build.

## Act one, what this is and why now

**Slide 1. Title.** Kicker SLALOM AUSTRALIA. Title: Value realisation. Turning technology investment into business outcomes we can prove. Keeps the existing title slide with the sharper subtitle.

**Slide 2. Executive summary.** Kicker THE ANSWER. Header: Launch value realisation as one integrated offering, proven in three lighthouse accounts this year. The slide carries the whole argument in four short blocks mirroring the acts: the problem and why now, what the discipline is, how it works, what we sell and do next. A reader who stops here has the full story. This replaces the two placeholder slides that asked for a problem statement, and it is written last, after every other slide is agreed.

**Slide 3. Why now.** Kicker WHY NOW. Header: Two thirds of boards now make further AI funding conditional on proof of value. Four verified figures with named sources on the slide: 66 per cent of boards make further AI funding conditional on proof of return and 43 per cent of finance leaders are being asked for a number they cannot produce, CloudZero 2026; 62 per cent of CFOs say fewer than a quarter of their AI initiatives deliver measurable benefit, Gartner May 2026; board level AI value reporting is expected to be standard for large enterprises by the end of 2026, Deloitte; fewer than a third of decision makers can tie AI value to financial growth and enterprises will defer a quarter of planned AI spend to 2027 as financial rigour increases, Forrester Predictions 2026. Every source is named on the slide, which answers the comment on the old why now slide about referencing.

**Slide 4. The problem.** Kicker THE PROBLEM. Header: Value is lost at the business case and after go live, the two points nobody manages. The three breaks from the old deck survive because the evidence now sits under each one: about a quarter of transformation value is lost at the business case and target setting stage, McKinsey 2021; delivery reports activity while 88 per cent of transformations miss their original ambition, Bain 2024 on more than twenty four thousand initiatives; benefits are checked after the recovery window has closed, with only 42 per cent of organisations mostly or always delivering full project benefits, Wellingtone 2026, and 89 per cent of operations leaders saying technology investments have not fully delivered, PwC 2026. This answers the comment that claims must be evidence backed.

**Slide 5. What value is.** Kicker DEFINE VALUE. Header: Value is a measured improvement in an outcome the business cares about; ROI is only one expression of it. The left side carries the working definition with its parts: expressed in the organisation's own terms, baselined before the change, owned by a named person in the operating business, due by a date, tied to a strategic priority, framed as an outcome rather than an output. The right side deals with ROI directly, because the room will conflate them: ROI is a ratio, so it hides scale and says nothing about which outcome moved; a use case can show strong ROI against a denominator the business never cared about; and productivity is a benefit currency the profit and loss account does not accept until something else changes. Gartner's own words carry the point, that a one size fits all valuation approach with a narrow focus on traditional financial metrics will significantly undervalue many investments, March 2026. Native units stay visible beside any financial translation.

**Slide 6. What value architecture and value realisation are.** Kicker DEFINE THE DISCIPLINE. Header: Value architecture designs the value an investment must create; value realisation makes it arrive and proves it. This is the slide the old deck never had and the reason the story felt unanchored. Two columns in plain english. Value architecture is the design half, done before and during the investment decision: the value case, the tree connecting the investment to the strategic priorities it drives, the baselines, the named owners, the measures in the board's own language, and the conditions that would kill it. Value realisation is the operating half, done through delivery and after go live: the behaviour and process change that produces the outcome, the tracking against baseline, the governance that intervenes, and the verification with finance that turns a claim into a booked result. One line ties them: architecture without realisation is a beautiful case that goes nowhere, and realisation without architecture is measurement with nothing agreed to measure against.

## Act two, how it works

**Slide 7. How an investment becomes value.** Kicker HOW VALUE IS CREATED. Header: The chain from investment to value usually breaks after the technology works. This rebuilds the chain slide the comments called confusing, and the fix is a worked example running under the chain so every link means something concrete. Example row, an AI assistant in claims handling: investment, the platform and change spend is committed; capability, the assistant is live in the workflow; adoption, four in five handlers actually use it; operating result, cycle time falls thirty per cent while rework stays flat, which is the quality check; business value, cost per claim falls and the freed capacity is redeployed to the backlog, and finance books the result. Under each link sits its failure mode: built and never used, used and nothing changes in the operating numbers, operating numbers move and the money never appears because nobody made the conversion decision. The lineage is named once, since this is Cranfield's benefits dependency logic, the one academically grounded causal chain in the field, and nobody has published an AI native version, which is ground we can own.

**Slide 8. The throughput trap.** Kicker MEASURE WHAT MATTERS. Header: Forty per cent of AI time savings disappear into rework before any value is booked. This is the new slide answering the question the deck has to answer, how we know what value the investment is driving when doing more is the claim. Three tests, each with its evidence. First, quality holds: pair every speed measure with a quality counterweight on the same population, the way DORA pairs software delivery throughput with stability, because its 2024 research found rising AI adoption associated with falling delivery stability, and Klarna's own arc proves the stakes, a forty million dollar assistant case publicly walked back by its CEO because cost was too predominant an evaluation factor and quality had fallen, before landing as a hybrid model. Second, the capacity lands somewhere priced: about 40 per cent of time saved with AI is immediately lost to correcting and reworking its outputs on Workday's 2025 research, roughly nine in ten executives report no firm level productivity impact at all on NBER's 2026 survey of six thousand executives, and McKinsey's finding is that saved time becomes earnings only when headcount, volume or price changes, so every use case must name its conversion lever and the reinvestment decision is the value moment. Third, finance can see it: the claim is baselined, attributed and booked, which is slide nine and ten's job. The teaching line for the room: hours saved is the weakest evidence of value that exists, and felt productivity is worse, since a controlled study found developers who believed AI made them twenty per cent faster were measured nineteen per cent slower.

**Slide 9. What makes a value claim credible.** Kicker MEASURE VALUE. Header: Six tests decide whether a value claim survives finance review. The six tests are promoted from the old appendix because measuring value is a main deck question: baseline, counterfactual, ownership, attribution, net value, verification. Attribution gets one concrete line so it never sounds academic: the clean method is a baseline plus a staggered rollout with a holdout, and it is genuinely doable, since the canonical study tracked five thousand support agents through a staggered AI deployment and measured throughput and quality together, and ANZ ran a hundred engineer controlled trial in weeks. The slide's closing line does the internal training work: if finance cannot see it in their numbers, we do not call it value yet.

**Slide 10. From idea to booked value.** Kicker TRACK VALUE. Header: Six gates separate forecast value from booked value. The six gates from the old deck are promoted: hypothesis, baselined, committed, delivered, realised, booked. The point the room must retain is the separation of forecast, observed and booked value, which is the discipline that stops value theatre. The gates also time phase expectations honestly, since adoption evidence arrives in weeks, operating outcomes in months and financial capture over a year or more, and demanding profit and loss proof at day ninety is as wrong as accepting adoption metrics at month twenty four.

**Slide 11. How value is realised.** Kicker REALISE VALUE. Header: A named value owner captures 24 per cent more of a transformation's value. The realisation half made concrete: every benefit has one owner inside the operating business, transformations with a dedicated value owner capture 24 per cent more of the value on Bain's evidence; the rhythm is weekly barrier removal, a monthly value review ending in a go, hold, kill or accelerate decision, and a quarterly executive review; and 70 per cent of AI value comes from redesigned people and process on BCG's rule, so realisation is behaviour work with measurement inside it, as opposed to a reporting function. The old six disciplines slide dissolves into this one and the governance detail moves to the appendix.

**Slide 12. The problems we see that clients don't.** Kicker WHAT WE SEE. Header: The same five failure patterns appear in almost every client we serve. Benefit tracking theatre, dashboards of numbers nobody owns; post project amnesia, the team disbands at go live and the benefit disbands with it; the wallpaper business case, signed at approval and irrelevant by month two; ownership by implication, a benefit with no name, no baseline and no consequence; and unmanaged freed capacity, the newest one, where AI creates real time savings that dissolve into rework and discretionary use because nobody made the reinvestment decision. Each pattern gets one sentence and each is the shadow of something the offering fixes, which is the bridge into act three.

## Act three, how we sell it

**Slide 13. The offering.** Kicker THE OFFERING. Header: We sell one discipline through three offers spanning the life of an investment. The liked slide, promoted and given the header its content deserved. Shape creates or resets the value case before funding or a gate; Realise runs the value discipline while delivery is live; Recover diagnoses platforms, AI and commitments the client already owns and returns a quantified recovery pool. One line under the three: the same definition of value, the same evidence tests and the same gates run through all three, which is what makes it one discipline rather than three products. The per offer detail slides move to the appendix as the comments suggested.

**Slide 14. The entry product.** Kicker THE ENTRY. Header: The value case review tests one live investment in ten days for a fixed fee. Day one confirms the decision and sponsors, days two to six test the value logic, baseline, ownership, costs and evidence, days seven to ten align the revised case and a ninety day plan. The executive receives four answers: where the case overclaims, which value pool is credible, what must change in ninety days, and whether to proceed, reshape, pause or recover. It expands naturally into Shape, Realise or Recover. Promoted up the deck as the comment asked, because the entry product is part of the sales story rather than the operating detail.

**Slide 15. The client conversation.** Kicker SELL VALUE. Header: Five questions shift the conversation from technology delivery to business value. The five questions in order: which strategic priority is this investment driving, as opposed to which technology outcome; which outcome must change and what is today's baseline; which behaviour or operating measure must move and who controls it; which technology capability enables that change; who owns the outcome and how will finance verify it. The footer carries the role logic that used to be its own slide: the business owner changes the outcome, finance verifies the claim, technology enables the evidence. The full role by role table moves to the appendix.

**Slide 16. Why us and where it attaches.** Kicker WHY SLALOM. Header: We win because the value evidence lives in the platforms we already build. Three attach points every account team can act on: every platform proposal carries a value case option, every programme at a gate or in trouble is an assurance conversation, every renewal or visibly underused platform is a recovery conversation. The differentiator stated once and plainly: strategy firms track value through manually reported programme status, vendors prove value before the sale and leave at signature, and we instrument adoption, operating measures and cost in the platforms as we build, which also moves the partner scorecards all four ecosystems now pay against. This gives the old why Slalom slide the purpose the comment found missing.

**Slide 17. What we do next.** Kicker THE ASK. Header: Three lighthouse accounts this year turn the thesis into referenceable proof. One thesis and one definition of value across the practice; three lighthouse clients chosen where a live decision, a trusted sponsor and accessible delivery evidence already exist; the Value Case Review launched as the published fixed fee entry; and the evidence, stories and delivery patterns productised as they repeat. The closing line returns to the spine: the prize is a differentiated route into strategy led platform work, and the proof standard we sell to clients is the standard we will hold ourselves to.

## The appendix

The appendix carries, in order: the seven value dimensions wheel; the Shape, Realise and Recover detail slides; the living value case operating view; the measurement architecture joining platform, operating and finance data; the governance cadence; the commercial model including the position on at risk fees; the account triggers and motion; the first year plan and team; the full stat bank with sources and confidence flags; and a one page glossary so value, value case, value architecture, value realisation, baseline, attribution and booked value each have one agreed sentence.

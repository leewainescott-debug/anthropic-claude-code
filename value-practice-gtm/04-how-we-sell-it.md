# How we sell it

Nobody buys value as an idea; in 2026 clients buy relief from a proof problem they already have. Boards are gating further AI funding on proof of return, finance leaders are being asked for return numbers 43 per cent of them cannot produce, CFOs are increasing technology spend while 86 per cent of their teams report no significant value from AI yet, and 74 per cent of CIOs believe their role is at risk within two years if they cannot demonstrate measurable gains. Selling value therefore means arriving with the problem named, evidenced and quantified before the client has asked, and with a small, safe, fixed price way to start. Everything in this motion follows from that.

## Opening the conversation when the client has not asked

The evidence on how demand gets created where no demand was expressed is consistent across three bodies of research. Teaching wins: the largest study of complex sales found top performers teach the client a problem or opportunity they did not know they had and that the seller is distinctively placed to solve, and that this outperforms relationship selling. Published thinking opens doors: 99 per cent of senior executives say a provider's published point of view matters when assessing them, and two thirds would decline to work with a new provider whose thinking is poor. And structured executive briefings convert: cross client research finds roughly three quarters of customers grow their spend after a well run briefing, with sales cycles shortening by a quarter or more.

So the motion runs in four steps, each creating the conditions for the next. First, publish the point of view, fronted by one named practitioner: the argument that realising value is the industry's measured weak point, that the loss concentrates at target setting and after go live, and that the proof now demanded at board level cannot be produced by the business cases organisations currently write. Second, take that point of view into a two hour executive briefing on the client's own situation, offered through the account teams into relationships Slalom already holds; the briefing teaches, benchmarks the client against the evidence base, and runs their most recent business case against the benefit quality gate, which is the moment the room watches its own case fail the test. Third, convert to the value case diagnostic, paid and fixed price, because a paid diagnostic is the consulting motion while free assessments are the vendor motion clients already receive and discount; the evidence says roughly one paid diagnostic in three converts to larger work. Fourth, expand along the lifecycle, into assurance, recovery or the office, and along the relationships the work itself creates, which is the subject of `05-beyond-the-cto.md`.

## Changing the conversation to be value led

The conversation changes when the question changes. The question a technology led engagement answers is what the platform will deliver, by when and for how much; the question a value led engagement answers is what this investment moves in the business as a whole, and how the client sets themselves up to realise it. In practice four questions do the reframing work in a first meeting, and they should be asked in this order: what strategic priority is this investment driving, as opposed to what technology outcome; what number on which executive's scorecard will move if it works, and who owns that number today; what was the baseline the day before the programme started, and would finance sign it; and what evidence, at what gate, would make you kill this. Most rooms cannot answer the second and fourth, and the Four Ares close the discussion cleanly, since are we getting the benefits is the question nobody present has been assigned.

The instrument of the reframe is the value case, replacing the business case whose whole job today is to get the dollars and then go nowhere. The reframe keeps its teeth through delivery because the case is rebaselined at every funding gate and money releases in tranches against evidence; and it ends deliberately, with benefits dissolving into owners' everyday scorecards after go live, because the research is clear that a tracking document kept alive forever becomes a gamed target and a reporting bureaucracy. The full argument for that refinement of the living case idea is in `09-where-we-disagree.md`.

## Pricing

Price each offer by the shape of what it is. The value case is a published fixed fee, recommended between sixty and ninety thousand dollars for three weeks; the figure sits inside the only real price evidence that exists, signed standalone benefits contracts running roughly forty thousand to two hundred and fifty thousand pounds and inferred diagnostic ranges of fifty to one hundred and fifty thousand dollars, and publishing it is itself a differentiator because no competitor in any tier publishes one. Value recovery diagnostics price the same way, and in the platforms arena they can often be part funded or fully funded through vendor programs, with the client paying for the enablement phase the roadmap converts into; the funding lines are laid out in `06-what-clients-actually-buy.md`. Value assurance prices by milestone against the gate cadence. The value office prices as a retainer, sized in embedded senior heads, which the public rate evidence puts at roughly one hundred and eighty to two hundred and fifty thousand pounds per head per year at the top of the range, with the build, operate and transfer arc priced as declining intensity.

On fees at risk, the position is patience by design. Roughly four in five executives say they want outcome elements in consulting contracts, and a quarter of McKinsey's global fees are now performance based, so the direction of the market is real; and the peer reviewed evidence says outcome contracts outperform only where outcomes are contractible, meaning measurable, attributable and pre agreed, while the practitioner evidence says pure gain share without clean baselines creates more disputes than it resolves. The practice's measurement work is precisely what makes outcomes contractible, which produces the commercial line worth saying to clients plainly: we will put fees at risk against numbers we baselined, on an attribution protocol we agreed at signing, and we are the party making that possible. Operationally that means hybrid structures, a fixed base with a capped at risk share of 10 to 20 per cent, offered selectively from year two on engagements where the practice built the baseline itself, and never at launch.

## What the account teams sell

The wider firm should carry three sentences. First, every platform proposal now includes a value case option, so the reframe travels inside work Slalom is already winning. Second, every delivery engagement at a gate or in trouble is an assurance referral. Third, every account where spend is up for renewal, or where a platform sits visibly underused, is a recovery referral. The rules of engagement, crediting and incentives that make account teams actually say those sentences are internal build matters, covered in `08-the-first-year.md`.
